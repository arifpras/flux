## The Pattern: Foreign Money in Declining Stocks

Every trading day, foreign investors buy and sell Indonesian stocks. When they accumulate shares during a price decline, it sends a signal: they see value where others see weakness.

Our analysis scanned 413 stocks with foreign buying in the past five trading days. Only 10 were declining in price. Among those, three stood out—not because foreigners were buying most, but because *smart money was buying the right ones*.

**Three stocks worth watching:**

### 1. **ADRO (Astra Agro Lestari) — The Energy Play** 🟢

**Price**: Rp 2,240 | **Change**: -2.2% (5 days) | **Jump**: +3.2% (today)

ADRO is Indonesia's largest coal producer, trading at a rock-bottom valuation. Its price-to-earnings ratio is 5.65—less than one-third the market average. Despite a 19% rally in January, it fell 2% over five days. That's where the real money entered.

**Why it matters:**
- Foreign investors accumulated 93.6 million shares—nearly half of all foreign buying in this entire group
- One foreign broker alone (UBS) bought 51% of all purchases. That's extreme institutional conviction
- The stock bounced 3.2% today on heavy volume, suggesting the bottom is in
- Return on equity of 11% means it actually makes money; many stocks this cheap don't

**The risk:** Energy stocks are cyclical. Coal demand could weaken if global growth slows.

**Entry strategy:** Buy at or below current levels (2,200-2,240). Target: 2,350-2,500 within 3-6 months.

### 2. **ASII (Astra International) — The Safe Play** 🟡

**Price**: Rp 7,275 | **Change**: -0.3% (5 days) | **Status**: Sideways

ASII is Indonesia's most valuable company. It's up 51% in the past year and 9% this month. Unlike ADRO, it isn't cheap—its valuation is fair. So why are foreign institutions buying?

**Why it matters:**
- State-owned Mandiri bank plus two foreign brokers (CLSA, Verdhana) are coordinating 86% of all purchases
- This combination signals institutional defence: states protect flagship companies
- The stock consolidates near highs (7,200-7,300 is the technical floor)
- Return on equity of 11% rivals ADRO but without cyclical risk

**The advantage:** ASII is Indonesia's blue chip—it's in every long-term portfolio. Safer than ADRO but slower growth potential.

**Entry strategy:** Buy on dips toward 7,250. Target: 7,400-7,750 within 3-6 months.

We searched for three conditions:
1. **Foreign buying** in the past 5 days (signal of smart money)

This eliminated 7 of the 10 declining stocks because they were either loss-making, overvalued, or illiquid.

## The Economist's View: Why This Matters

**Institutional investors don't time bottoms perfectly.** But when they accumulate heavily at low valuations while retail investors panic-sell, history favors the holders.

ADRO shows the classic pattern: extreme concentration by one major foreign player (UBS), rock-bottom valuation (PER 5.65), confirming technical reversal (+3.2% bounce), and immediate reinforcement (volume surge). This is how institutional accumulation campaigns look in real-time.

ASII shows coordination—not by accident, but by institutional design. State funds and foreign long-term capital rarely align unless conviction is high.

**The timing is right** because both stocks rallied significantly (ADRO +19%, ASII +9%) before a normal pullback. Foreign investors are buying the dip in strong stocks, not catching falling knives.

## How to Do This Yourself

The data comes from three sources:

1. **Foreign buying data** (from IDX Ringkasan Histories)
   - Available free from Indonesian Exchange
   - Updated daily after market close

2. **Fundamental data** (from IDX Stock Screener)
   - Price/Earnings ratio, Price/Book ratio
   - Return on Equity, Profit margin
   - Available free on idx.co.id

3. **Broker flow data** (Concentration of buying)
   - Shows who's accumulating (institutions) vs distributing (retail)
   - Requires broker summaries from exchange

**The screening takes 30 minutes** with a spreadsheet. Anyone can do this.
