STRATEGY #1: HARVEST DIVIDEND INCOME
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHO IT'S FOR: Income investors, retirees, anyone who needs cash flow

THE SETUP: Some Indonesian stocks pay staggering dividends—50% to 100%+ yields 
over the past three years. These aren't mistakes in the data. They're real 
companies distributing substantial profits (or liquidating, in some cases).

TOP PICKS:

ADRO (114% yield, 8 payments): Coal producer riding energy demand. Made eight 
dividend distributions in three years—that's reliability. Current price: 2,350.

PTBA (72% yield, 3 payments): Another coal play, fewer but larger payments. 
Still trading at 2,530, well off its highs.

BSSR (50% yield, 9 payments): Logistics company with the most consistent 
payment schedule—nine distributions shows operational stability. Price: 3,940.

ITMG (66% yield, 6 payments): Coal mining company with regular semi-annual 
dividends. Trading at 22,725.

LPPF (55% yield, 3 payments): Department store operator pivoting to property. 
Riskier business model but solid dividends. Price: 1,865.

HOW TO TRADE IT:

Buy 2-3 weeks before expected ex-dividend dates (check IDX calendar). Hold 
through payment. Reinvest or take cash—your call. Don't chase the highest 
yields blindly; SCPI at 317% only made two payments and may not continue.

Focus on frequency: 6+ payments in three years signals sustainability. Avoid 
one-off special dividends unless you're selling before the post-dividend drop.

EXPECTED RETURN: 10-15% annual cash yield plus 0-10% capital appreciation if 
commodity prices cooperate. Main risk is dividend cuts during downturns.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHO IT'S FOR: Value investors comfortable with 3-6 month holds

THE SETUP: Foreign institutions are buying 25 stocks even as prices fall. This 
pattern—accumulation during weakness—often precedes recoveries. Either they 
know something or they're wrong, but history favors the former.

TOP PICKS:

BRMS (139M shares, -1.6%): Pharma company with largest foreign accumulation. 
Modest decline suggests controlled buying. Price: 1,230.

ASII (52M shares, -4.9%): Astra International, the conglomerate. Down 5% but 
foreign institutions adding 52 million shares. Price: 6,775.

PTRO (39M shares, -13.1%): Oil & gas driller hit harder (-13%) but still 
attracting capital. Higher risk, higher potential. Price: 10,775.

CTRA (30M shares, -1.7%): Property developer seeing steady accumulation. 
Price: 890.

KLBF (21M shares, -0.4%): Pharmaceuticals, minimal price decline with 
institutional interest. Price: 1,225.

HOW TO TRADE IT:

Buy when foreign accumulation exceeds 10M shares AND price is down 2-10% over 
five days. Avoid extreme drops (>20%) unless you understand the specific 
catalyst. Set initial stops at -7% from entry. Hold 3-6 months or until price 
recovers to pre-decline levels plus 10%.

Watch for continuation: If foreign buying persists for 2-3 weeks, add to 
positions. If it stops, reassess.

EXPECTED RETURN: 15-30% over 3-6 months if the thesis plays out. Risk is 
institutions are early (you'll sit through more declines) or wrong (company has 
real problems).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHO IT'S FOR: Conservative value investors, institutional allocators

THE SETUP: Take Strategy #2 but add fundamental screens. Only buy companies 
with ROE >8%, profit margins >5%, P/E ratios under 25, and manageable debt. 
This filters out 75% of the names, leaving 25 solid businesses temporarily 
unloved.

TOP PICKS:

ASII (52M shares, -4.9%): Still makes the cut with strong fundamentals. 
Diversified conglomerate, proven management. Price: 6,775.

KLBF (21M shares, -0.4%): Pharma with consistent profitability and reasonable 
valuation. Price: 1,225.

UNTR (2.4M shares, -12.9%): Heavy equipment and mining services. Sharp decline 
but solid business. Price: 27,450.

GMFI (56M shares, -14.5%): Bank facing short-term pressures but fundamentals 
intact. Price: 71.

CNMA (36M shares, -1.6%): Cinema operator recovering post-pandemic, now 
attracting foreign interest. Price: 121.

HOW TO TRADE IT:

This is the highest-conviction subset. Allocate larger position sizes (up to 
10% of portfolio per name) because you have both technical and fundamental 
support. Buy on weakness, hold through recovery, sell when price reaches fair 
value (typically 20-40% above entry).

Use fundamental deterioration as your stop, not arbitrary price levels. If 
quarterly earnings show margin compression or ROE decline, exit regardless of 
price.

EXPECTED RETURN: 20-40% over 6-12 months with lower risk than pure technical 
plays. These are businesses you can defend in a portfolio review.

WHO IT'S FOR: Active traders, anyone who checks positions daily

catching the move—you're riding the second or third wave.

BBKP (132M shares, +3.6%): Gained hard after institutional buying. Look for

PSKT (25M shares, +4.7%): Already up 4.7%, watch for +7-10% move. Current: 402.

ELTY (20M shares, +10.8%): Biggest gainer, may be overextended but has

ELIT (13M shares, +9.0%): Tech play with strong momentum, room to run.

PBRX (8M shares, +9.8%): Pharma with sharp move, entering momentum phase.

Enter same-day or next morning after signal emerges. Tight stops: 2-3% below

This strategy demands discipline. You'll have more losing trades (40-50% win

EXPECTED RETURN: +15-25% annually if you execute well, but requires active

STRATEGY #5: TECHNICAL SETUPS (BOLLINGER BANDS)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

WHO IT'S FOR: Swing traders comfortable with 1-4 week holds

THE SETUP: Eighteen stocks are technically oversold—trading near the bottom of 
their Bollinger Bands—while maintaining upward trends. This is the classic 
"buy the dip in an uptrend" setup. When price touches the lower band, it 
typically bounces within 3-7 days.

TOP PICKS:

BANK (800, MA10: 957): Deep oversold (-9% below band), strong mean-reversion 
setup. Volatility: 4.7%.

DGIK (140, MA10: 161): Sitting 7% below band, clean trend structure. 
Volatility: 4.8%.

UNTR (27,450, MA10: 30,662): Only 3% below band, lower-risk entry. 
Volatility: 3.8%.

MERK (3,260, MA10: 3,322): Barely oversold (-1.6%), tightest setup. 
Volatility: 0.8%—very stable.

ALII (905, MA10: 1,106): Early-stage oversold, room to develop. Volatility: 5.8%.

HOW TO TRADE IT:

Buy when price hits 0-30% of Bollinger Band range. Enter with limit orders at 
the lower band price. Exit at 70-100% of range (upper band) or after +10-15% 
gain. Stop loss: -3% from entry.

This works in range-bound or mildly bullish markets. It fails during crashes 
when price stays oversold for weeks. Check market regime before deploying.

Best conditions: Low overall market volatility, no macro shocks imminent, stock 
has clear support levels.

EXPECTED RETURN: +8-15% per trade over 1-4 weeks. Win rate around 60-65% if 
you wait for proper setups. Don't force trades when Bollinger Bands are too 
tight or too wide.

BUILDING YOUR PORTFOLIO
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

YOU DON'T HAVE TO CHOOSE ONE STRATEGY

Most investors do best running multiple approaches:

30% → Dividend income (Strategy 1)
     Pick: ADRO, PTBA, BSSR
     Why: Steady cash flow, low maintenance, holds through volatility

25% → Fundamental contrarian (Strategy 3)  
     Pick: ASII, KLBF, UNTR
     Why: Core value holdings with catalyst potential

25% → Technical + fundamentals (Strategy 6)
     Pick: DGIK, UNTR (double up), MERK, SUNI
     Why: Tactical trades with fundamental safety

20% → Short-term momentum (Strategy 4)
     Pick: Rotate through fresh signals weekly
     Why: Capture quick moves, stay opportunistic

This allocation gives you:
- Income from dividends (reduces need to sell in downturns)
- Value exposure (compounds wealth over years)
- Technical timing (improves entry points by 5-10%)
- Momentum optionality (participates in strong moves)


POSITION SIZING RULES

Conservative investors (low risk tolerance):
- 3% per dividend stock, 5% per value stock, 2% per technical setup
- Maximum 15 positions total
- Keep 25% cash for opportunities

Aggressive traders (high risk tolerance):
- 5% per dividend stock, 8% per value stock, 5% per technical setup
- Maximum 20 positions, rotate freely
- Keep 10% cash, deploy rapidly

The key: Size according to strategy conviction and your edge. You should have 
higher win rates on fundamental value than momentum trades, so size the former 
larger even if returns are slower.


WHAT COULD GO WRONG

MARKET CRASH: Technical strategies fail entirely. Dividend stocks hold up best, 
fundamental value second. Momentum bleeds hardest.

LIQUIDITY CRISIS: Small-cap dividend payers (TMAS, SCPI) become untradeable. 
Stick to large-caps (ADRO, UNTR, ASII) if worried about exits.

FOREIGN OUTFLOWS: If institutions reverse and become net sellers, Strategy 2/3 
face headwinds. Monitor weekly flow data; if negative for 3+ weeks, reduce 
exposure.

COMMODITY COLLAPSE: Coal dividend plays (ADRO, PTBA, ITMG) get crushed. 
Diversify income sources—add non-commodity names like BSSR and LPPF.

EARNINGS MISSES: Fundamental screens use backward-looking data. A company can 
pass all filters and then report terrible quarterly results. Check earnings 
calendars before entries.


EXECUTION CHECKLIST

BEFORE YOU BUY:
□ Verify stock isn't suspended or under investigation (check IDX announcements)
□ Confirm adequate liquidity (>500M IDR daily average volume)
□ Check earnings calendar (avoid day-before-earnings entries for value plays)
□ Review chart for obvious resistance levels above your entry
□ Set profit target and stop loss before opening position

POSITION MANAGEMENT:
□ Log entry price, date, strategy type in spreadsheet
□ Set calendar reminders for technical exits (5-7 days out)
□ Review value holdings monthly for fundamental deterioration
□ Take partial profits at +8-10%, let remainder run to target
□ Cut all losses at predetermined stops—no exceptions, no hope

PORTFOLIO REVIEW (WEEKLY):
□ Rerun screens to find fresh opportunities (especially momentum)
□ Check if existing positions still pass entry criteria (if not, consider exit)
□ Measure actual returns vs expected returns by strategy
□ Adjust allocation if one strategy consistently underperforms


THE EDGE

These strategies work because Indonesian equity markets remain inefficient:

Information asymmetry: Foreign institutions get research and management access 
before retail. Following their flows puts you closer to informed capital.

Dividend culture: Indonesian companies distribute cash generously, but retail 
investors overlook ex-dates and yield calculations. You won't.

Technical gaps: Most local investors don't use Bollinger Bands or systematic 
momentum tracking. Your edge is discipline and quantitative signals.

Fundamental screening: Many traders chase momentum without checking if 
businesses are profitable. Combining fundamental filters with technical timing 
is still an underused approach.

The real question isn't whether these edges exist—they do. The question is 
whether you'll execute systematically when signals trigger, or freeze and 
second-guess yourself.
