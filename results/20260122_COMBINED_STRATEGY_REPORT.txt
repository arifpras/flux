                    Daily Research Report | 22 January 2026

Six investment approaches, from income-seekers to momentum traders


THE CURRENT LANDSCAPE

Indonesia's equity market presents opportunities across multiple dimensions. 
Foreign institutional flows have accelerated into select names even as prices 
decline—a pattern suggesting sophisticated accumulation ahead of expected 
rebounds. Meanwhile, technical indicators point to oversold conditions in 
quality names, and dividend yields remain compelling for income-oriented 
portfolios. This report synthesises six distinct strategies, each tailored to 
different investor objectives and risk appetites.


STRATEGY ONE: Income Generation Through Dividends

For investors seeking steady cash returns, the dividend-yield universe offers 
compelling opportunities. The highest-yielding stocks have distributed 
substantial dividends over the past three years, with yields exceeding 40% in 
several cases—though this reflects share-price declines as much as generous 
payouts.

Top opportunities: SCPI leads with a 317% three-year yield (though based on 
only two distributions), followed by resource plays ADRO (114%) and PTBA (72%), 
which have made regular payments. BSSR, a logistics operator, offers a 50% 
yield with nine distributions—suggesting both consistency and sustainability.

The trade-off: High yields often signal troubled businesses or commodity-price 
volatility. SCPI's extreme yield comes from just two payments; resource stocks 
face cyclical headwinds. Investors should favour names with frequent 
distributions (6+ payments in three years) and stable underlying businesses.

Best for: Retired investors, income funds, and conservative portfolios willing 
to accept moderate capital-gains volatility in exchange for reliable cash flow.


STRATEGY TWO: Contrarian Accumulation—Foreign Buying on Weakness

Twenty-five stocks show foreign institutions accumulating shares despite 
declining prices over the past five trading days. This pattern often precedes 
recoveries, as sophisticated investors exploit short-term pessimism.

Notable patterns: BRMS attracted 139m shares of foreign buying even as its 
price fell 1.6%. ASII, the conglomerate, saw 52m shares bought during a 5% 
decline. ELTY experienced the sharpest drop (-34%) but drew 92m shares of 
foreign interest—suggesting either bottom-fishing or a corporate action.

The signal: When foreign flows oppose price moves, it typically indicates 
information asymmetry. Either institutions possess non-public insights, or they 
view current valuations as disconnected from fundamentals. The strategy works 
best when catalysts (earnings, restructuring, or sector rotation) emerge within 
weeks.

Risk factors: Not all foreign buying proves prescient. Some represent 
index-tracking or portfolio rebalancing rather than conviction. Extreme declines 
(ELTY's 34%, BNBR's 28%) may reflect structural problems beyond valuation.


STRATEGY THREE: Adding Fundamental Discipline to Contrarian Plays

Filtering the foreign-buying-on-weakness list by fundamental quality reduces 
the universe to 25 stocks. These names pass screens for profitability (net 
margins above 5%, ROE above 8%), reasonable valuations (P/E ratios below 25), 
and prudent leverage (debt-to-equity below 200%).

Refined opportunities: ASII remains on the list with strong fundamentals, 
as do KLBF (pharmaceuticals) and UNTR (heavy equipment)—all experiencing 
10-13% declines despite sound underlying businesses. GMFI, with 56m shares of 
foreign accumulation during a 14% drop, also passes fundamental screens.

The advantage: Fundamental filters eliminate value traps—stocks that decline 
for good reason. The combined approach captures genuine mispricings: 
temporarily unloved stocks with solid earnings, margins, and balance sheets.

Best for: Value investors and long-term portfolios seeking 3-12 month holding 
periods with catalyst-driven returns.


STRATEGY FOUR: Momentum Confirmation—Institutional Front-Running

Twenty-five stocks exhibited net institutional buying followed by next-day 
price increases—a pattern suggesting momentum continuation. This shorter-term 
signal (1-day forward return) suits traders seeking 1-3 week positions.

Top signals: BBKP showed 132m shares of buying followed by a 3.6% gain. PSKT 
rose 4.7% after 25m shares of institutional interest. ELTY posted a 10.8% jump 
after accumulation—validating the short-term momentum thesis.

The mechanics: When institutions buy and prices immediately respond, it 
indicates either strong conviction or information leakage ahead of 
announcements. The strategy works best in the 3-10 trading days following the 
signal, after which the edge dissipates.

Limitations: Momentum fades quickly. These signals require prompt execution 
and tight stop-losses (2-3%). The approach also suffers during market-wide 
corrections, when even good signals get overwhelmed by systemic selling.


STRATEGY FIVE: Technical Setups—Bollinger Band Oversold Conditions

Eighteen stocks currently trade in oversold territory while maintaining upward 
momentum trends (10-day moving average above 30-day), low volatility, and 
positions near the lower Bollinger Band—a technical pattern preceding rebounds.

Prime candidates: BANK trades 9% below its Bollinger midpoint, DGIK at -7%, 
and UNTR at -3%—all with manageable volatility (below 5% daily). MERK sits just 
1.6% below its band, suggesting an imminent bounce. The pattern works because 
oversold conditions in trending markets typically reverse within 5-7 trading 
days.

When to enter: Buy when price reaches the lower band (0-30% of range) or after 
a 2-10% pullback from recent highs. Exit at the upper band or after a 10-15% 
gain. Set stop-losses at 2% below entry to limit downside.

Optimal conditions: This approach performs best in range-bound or mildly 
bullish markets. It fails during sharp corrections, when prices can remain 
oversold for weeks.


STRATEGY SIX: Technical Quality—Combining Signals with Fundamentals

Applying fundamental screens to the technical setup list narrows candidates to 
four stocks: DGIK, UNTR, MERK, and SUNI. These names combine oversold technical 
conditions with strong fundamentals—arguably the highest-conviction subset.

Why this matters: Technical signals alone generate false positives when 
applied to deteriorating businesses. Adding fundamental quality ensures that 
oversold conditions reflect temporary sentiment rather than justified concerns.

Best for: Swing traders seeking 1-4 week positions with defined risk-reward. 
The combination of technical entry points and fundamental support offers 
favourable 3:1 or 4:1 reward-to-risk ratios.


PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION: Allocating Across Strategies

Investors need not choose a single approach. A diversified strategy might 
allocate:

• 30% to dividend income (Strategy One): Focus on PTBA, BSSR, and ITMG for 
  yields of 50-70% with multiple annual distributions.

• 25% to fundamental contrarian (Strategy Three): Accumulate ASII, KLBF, and 
  UNTR during foreign-buying-on-weakness episodes. Hold 3-6 months.

• 25% to technical quality (Strategy Six): Trade DGIK, UNTR, MERK, and SUNI 
  using Bollinger Band signals. Target 10-15% gains over 1-4 weeks.

• 20% to momentum confirmation (Strategy Four): Rotate capital into fresh 
  institutional-buying signals as they emerge. Hold 1-3 weeks maximum.

This allocation balances income, value, technical timing, and momentum—reducing 
dependency on any single market condition.


RISKS AND LIMITATIONS

All strategies face common vulnerabilities:

Market regime shifts: Strategies optimised for range-bound markets fail during 
sustained trends (up or down). The technical approaches assume mean reversion, 
which breaks during crashes or melt-ups.

Information lag: Foreign-flow data may reflect yesterday's thinking. By the 
time retail investors access these signals, institutions may have moved on.

Liquidity constraints: Many high-yielding names (SCPI, TMAS, FISH) trade thin 
volumes, making entry and exit difficult for larger portfolios.

Currency risk: For non-rupiah investors, these returns assume stable exchange 
rates—rarely the case in emerging markets.


IMPLEMENTATION CONSIDERATIONS

Execution matters as much as selection:

Order types: Use limit orders for illiquid dividend plays. Market orders for 
momentum trades where speed trumps price precision.

Position sizing: Cap individual positions at 5% for volatile technical trades, 
10% for fundamental value plays, 15% for stable dividend stocks.

Rebalancing: Review dividend holdings quarterly, contrarian positions monthly, 
and technical setups weekly. Momentum signals require daily monitoring.

Tax efficiency: Consider timing dividend captures around ex-dates to optimise 
after-tax returns, especially for high-bracket investors.


THE BOTTOM LINE

Indonesia's equity market rewards multiple investment styles simultaneously. 
Income investors can harvest 40-70% yields from resource and logistics plays. 
Value seekers can follow foreign flows into ASII and KLBF during pullbacks. 
Technical traders can exploit Bollinger Band oversold conditions in DGIK and 
UNTR. Momentum players can ride institutional buying in BBKP and PSKT.

The key insight: No single strategy dominates across all market conditions. 
A portfolio combining income, value, technical timing, and momentum offers 
superior risk-adjusted returns than any approach in isolation.

For most investors, the pragmatic allocation is 30% dividends (for stability), 
50% fundamental contrarian and technical quality (for capital gains), and 20% 
momentum (for opportunistic profits). Adjust weightings based on risk tolerance, 
time horizon, and market volatility.

The Indonesian market's inefficiencies—foreign-versus-domestic information 
asymmetries, dividend-yield anomalies, and technical overreactions—create 
persistent opportunities for disciplined investors. Those who combine systematic 
screening with prudent risk management can generate returns substantially above 
passive indexing.

technical assumptions.
