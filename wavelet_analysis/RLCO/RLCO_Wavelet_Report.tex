\section{Wavelet Method Explanation}\label{wavelet-method-explanation}

\subsection{What is Wavelet Analysis?}\label{what-is-wavelet-analysis}

Wavelet analysis is a mathematical technique that decomposes price
movements into components at different time scales. Unlike traditional
moving averages that smooth data uniformly, wavelets preserve sharp
market reversals and changes while removing noise.

Think of it as having three synchronized traders watching the market: -
\textbf{Trader 1} watches daily price changes (short-term timing) -
\textbf{Trader 2} tracks 3-5 day momentum (medium-term confirmation)\\
- \textbf{Trader 3} monitors weekly trends (long-term direction)

When all three traders agree the market is moving up, confidence is
maximum.

\subsection{How RLCO Analysis Works}\label{how-rlco-analysis-works}

The analysis uses a \textbf{Morlet wavelet}---the gold standard for
financial markets---applied across 31 different scales covering roughly
1 to 32-day periodicities. The data is decomposed into three primary
components:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Short-term component} (Scale \textasciitilde8): Daily
  entry/exit signals for active traders
\item
  \textbf{Medium-term component} (Scale \textasciitilde16): Multi-day
  momentum confirmation\\
\item
  \textbf{Long-term component} (Scale \textasciitilde24): Weekly
  directional bias
\end{enumerate}

For each trading day, the algorithm checks whether each component is
positive (bullish) or negative (bearish). The final signal combines all
three:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{STRONG BUY (3/3)}: All three timeframes bullish → Execute
  trades with confidence
\item
  \textbf{BUY (2/3)}: Two timeframes bullish → Consider entry with
  caution
\item
  \textbf{NEUTRAL (1/3)}: Mixed signals → Wait for clearer direction
\item
  \textbf{SELL (0/3)}: All timeframes bearish → Avoid or exit positions
\end{itemize}
